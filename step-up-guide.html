📋 Florida Step-Up

How Florida Step-Up for Students actually works for homeschool co-ops

By Rajeev, The Grow Co-op 📅 March 2026 ⏱ 8 min read

If you run a Florida homeschool co-op, you've almost certainly dealt with Step-Up for Students — probably by spending hours every billing cycle downloading portal exports, manually entering payment amounts, and hoping you haven't missed a disbursement. This guide explains how the program actually works from an administrator's perspective, what the payment flow looks like in practice, and what you need to get right to stay compliant.

Who this is for

Co-op administrators and microschool operators who accept Step-Up EMA (Educational Multipurpose Account) scholarships. This covers the administrative side — not the parent application process.

What Step-Up for Students actually is

Step-Up for Students is a Florida nonprofit that administers several scholarship programs on behalf of the state. For homeschool co-ops and microschools, the relevant program is the Family Empowerment Scholarship for Educational Options (FES-EO), which funds an Educational Multipurpose Account — the "EMA" you'll see in your portal exports.

The EMA works like a spending account: parents are awarded a dollar amount each year that they can use for qualifying educational expenses. Your co-op submits invoices through the Step-Up portal, parents approve them, and Step-Up disburses payment to you directly — typically via ACH to your bank account.

Important distinction

Step-Up pays you, not the family. The disbursement comes from Step-Up's payment processor (Tipalti) to your bank. The family never touches the money — they just approve the invoice in their portal.

The payment flow, step by step

  1. 1
    You create an invoice in the Step-Up provider portal for each student's tuition or fees. Invoices are tied to specific students by their EMA account number — the same number you'll see as the "student sequence number" in portal exports.
  2. 2
    The parent approves the invoice in their Step-Up family portal. Until they approve it, nothing happens. This is the most common source of payment delays — parents who haven't logged in to approve outstanding invoices.
  3. 3
    Step-Up processes the payment through Tipalti, their payment processor. Processing typically takes 5–10 business days after parent approval.
  4. 4
    The disbursement hits your bank account as an ACH transfer. Each disbursement corresponds to a specific line item in the portal, tied to a specific student and invoice.
  5. 5
    You reconcile the payment against your family's tuition balance — crediting the right student's account, attributed to the right program.

Steps 1–4 are handled through the Step-Up portal. Step 5 is where most co-ops struggle, because by the time disbursements arrive in your bank account, you've lost the context of which student they were for.

Understanding your portal export

The Step-Up provider portal lets you export a tab-delimited file of all your payment history. This is the key file for reconciliation. Here are the columns you actually need to understand:

ColumnWhat it meansWhy it matters
StudentSequenceNumberThe student's unique identifier in the Step-Up systemThis is how you match a disbursement to the right student in your records. Don't rely on names — they can have typos.
CategoryDetailNameThe type of expense the invoice was filed under"Tuition" maps to your main academic program. "Other Fees" typically covers enrichment fees like workshops or electives.
PurchaseAmountThe amount disbursedMay differ from your invoice amount if the student's EMA balance was insufficient.

DatePaidWhen the disbursement was madeUse this as the payment date when posting to your records — not the invoice date.

Common gotcha

The portal export includes all statuses by default — Pending, Cancelled, and Paid. If you import everything without filtering by Status = "Paid", you'll overstate your revenue. Always filter to Paid-only before reconciling.

How program attribution works

If your co-op runs multiple programs — a core academic program and enrichment classes, for example — you need to know which Step-Up payment belongs to which program. The CategoryDetailName column is your signal:

  • "Tuition" — this maps to your main academic tuition. Post to your core program.
  • "Other Fees" — this maps to enrichment fees, workshops, or elective programs billed as additional charges.
  • Other values (Materials, Technology, etc.) — depends on what your families are claiming. If you only bill tuition and program fees, you generally won't see these.

The attribution isn't perfect — Step-Up doesn't know which specific class a "Tuition" payment is for, only that it's academic tuition. If a student is enrolled in multiple programs, you'll need to decide internally how to allocate it. Most co-ops attribute "Tuition" to the core program and "Other Fees" to enrichment, which works well in practice.

Invoicing requirements and compliance

Step-Up has specific requirements for what your invoices must contain to be reimbursable. The details can shift as the program evolves, so always check the current provider agreement — but the consistent requirements are:

  • The invoice must be on your organization's letterhead with your EIN and contact information
  • The service period must be specified (e.g., "August 2025 – May 2026")
  • Line items must be described as educational services (not "tuition invoice #1052")
  • The invoice must not include non-educational costs — field trips, fundraising, optional donations, or anything that isn't a direct educational service
  • The student's name must appear on the invoice

Family statements you generate for Step-Up documentation purposes should include a compliance note — something like: "All services listed are educational in nature and comply with Step Up For Students' eligibility guidelines. No portion of this fee includes fundraising, optional donations, or non-educational costs." Having this language on every statement saves time if a family is ever audited.

Practical tip

Keep a copy of every invoice you submit to the portal, and a record of every Step-Up payment you receive, tied to the specific student and invoice. Step-Up providers are subject to audits, and having clean records is your protection.

The reconciliation problem

Here's where most co-ops get bogged down. By the time a Step-Up disbursement arrives in your bank account, it's a line item that says something like "Tipalti Inc" with an amount. You have to match it back to a specific student, a specific invoice, and a specific program — and post it against that family's outstanding balance.

If you're doing this manually, the workflow looks like:

  1. Export the payment history from the Step-Up portal
  2. Cross-reference each paid item against your family records by student name or sequence number
  3. Post each payment to the correct family's ledger in whatever system you're using
  4. Mark the corresponding invoice as paid
  5. Repeat when new disbursements arrive (usually 1–2 times per month during the school year)

For a co-op with 10 families and 15 students, this takes 1–2 hours per reconciliation cycle. With 25+ families across multiple programs, it can eat half a day — and errors are easy to make when you're manually matching names and amounts.

What a clean reconciliation process looks like

The cleanest approach is to match by student sequence number rather than name. Names have typos and formatting differences ("María" vs "Maria", "Jr." vs no suffix). Sequence numbers are consistent.

For each billing cycle:

  1. Export from the Step-Up portal — all history, not just the current period
  2. Filter to Status = "Paid" only
  3. Match each record to your student roster by sequence number
  4. Check CategoryDetailName to attribute to the right program
  5. Use the composite key (TipaltiPaymentNumber + LineItemNumber + BusinessInvoiceNumber) to deduplicate against records you've already posted — re-exporting is safe if you do this
  6. Post matched payments to family balances
  7. Flag any unmatched records for manual review

Doing this in a spreadsheet is workable at small scale. Once you have more than 15–20 families with multiple Step-Up students, the manual process becomes error-prone enough to warrant a dedicated tool.
