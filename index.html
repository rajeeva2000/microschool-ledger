Why generic tools fail

Generic billing software wasn't designed for how microschools actually work.

Most billing tools assume one customer, one invoice, one payment stream. Microschools and co-ops are messier — and much more human.

You bill families, not students

The paying relationship is with the family, but the services are delivered to individual students across different programs with sibling discount rules.

Direct payments and scholarships collide

A family might pay by check while Florida Step-Up sends a separate disbursement days later. Both need to land against the same balance without manual guesswork.

Your school year doesn't match the calendar year

Deposits for next year can arrive while you're still collecting this year’s monthly tuition. General-purpose accounting tools don’t understand that overlap.

Your administrator is also your teacher

You don’t have a billing department. You need a system that feels calm, clear, and usable between classes — not enterprise software that adds more cognitive load.

Built for enrichment providers & elective programs

Flat fees, semester terms, and workshop charges — alongside annual tuition, in the same system.

Not every school runs on annual tuition. Add a $300 Writing Workshop fee or a $50 materials charge as a flat charge alongside any enrollment. Or flag a program as short-term and run it by term (Fall 2026, Spring 2027) with a flat fee per session — complete with a roster view showing enrolled vs. paid per term.

  • Flat charges: per-student or family-level fees with due dates — no separate reconciliation
  • Short-term programs: term-based enrollment with flat fees, not annual payment grids
  • Both models coexist in one school — annual tuition families and semester-fee students on the same platform
54% of Florida's 2,921 Step-Up providers are enrichment or elective programs. Microschool Ledger now handles both flat fees and semester courses for that majority.

Built for private schools using Step-Up

The accounting records your Step-Up AUP requires — ready before the auditor asks.

Florida statute requires every private school receiving Step-Up scholarship funds to demonstrate an adequate accounting system, proper classification of scholarship deposits, and documented educational expenditures. Schools receiving more than $250,000 must contract a licensed CPA for a formal AUP engagement under Florida Statute 1002.421(1)(q). Microschool Ledger's P&L, expense tracking, Step-Up reconciliation, and audit log satisfy all three criteria — and the Export for CPA button generates the exact workbook your auditor needs.

  • AUP Compliance tab: auto-checks whether school info, expenses, and scholarship records are complete
  • Export for CPA: one-click Excel workbook (AUP Summary, Scholarship Funds, Expense Detail by Category)
  • Amber threshold warning when your Step-Up total approaches the $250,000 CPA-required level
$250K The Step-Up threshold that triggers a mandatory CPA audit under Florida Statute 1002.421(1)(q). MSLedger gives your auditor a clean, complete record to work from.
