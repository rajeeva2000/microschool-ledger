Attendance guide

How to take attendance at a microschool or homeschool co-op (with a free sign-in sheet)

By Microschool Ledger November 2026 6 min read

In a small school, attendance usually starts as a clipboard by the door. That works until a parent asks which days their child missed last month, a scholarship program asks for records, or two staff members remember the same morning differently. This guide covers what to record, how to handle the messy cases, and how to keep records you can actually find later.

What to record every school day

You don't need much. For each student, on each day your school meets, record:

  • The date, and only on days you actually meet. A Tuesday-Thursday program shouldn't show Mondays as absences.
  • A status: Present, Absent or Excused. Three choices are enough for most schools, and they're easy for any staff member to apply the same way.
  • Arrival time, if the student came late.
  • Departure time and who picked them up, if the student left early.
  • A short note when something needs explaining ("doctor's appointment," "field trip day").
  • Who recorded it, so you can ask the right person if a day looks wrong later.
Check your own requirements

Rules differ by state and by type of program. Florida private schools, for example, are expected to keep attendance records, while many homeschool co-ops support parents who keep their own. Scholarship programs may have their own expectations too. Check what applies to your school; the routine below works either way.

Late arrivals, early pickups and excused absences

Late arrivals

Mark the student Present and write down the arrival time. Keeping it as a time, not a separate "Late" status, lets you see patterns ("always 20 minutes late on Thursdays") without making your totals harder to read.

Early pickups

Record the time and the name of the adult who picked the student up. This is the entry you'll be most glad to have if a question ever comes up. Keep a list of the adults each family has approved for pickup, and check it before a child leaves with someone new.

Excused vs. absent

Decide what counts as excused (illness, a family emergency, an appointment the parent told you about in advance) and write it down once, in your family handbook. Then every staff member applies the same rule, and families know what to expect.

Days off and short weeks

Holidays and teacher workdays aren't absences. Leave them off your sheet entirely rather than marking everyone absent.

    Have the list ready before drop-offPrint the sheet below with names already filled in, or open your attendance app on a phone or tablet by the door.
  2. 2
    Mark students as they arriveA parent initial at drop-off is a simple extra check for younger students.
  3. 3
    Finish the day's statuses after drop-offAnyone not there yet is Absent or Excused for now. If they arrive later, change them to Present and add the time.
  4. 4
    Note early pickups as they happenTime out and who picked them up, checked against the family's pickup list.
  5. 5
    Close out the dayIf you used paper, enter it into your records the same day, while everyone still remembers. Then file or photograph the sheet.

Keeping records you can find later

Attendance records matter months after the fact: a family asks about their child's missed days, you prepare a year-end summary, or you just want to see how attendance changed across the year. A few habits make that easy:

  • One place, not three. A sheet in a binder, a group text and a spreadsheet each holding part of the story is how records get lost.
  • Correct, don't erase. If a day was recorded wrong, fix it and keep a note of the change.
  • Keep students who leave. Archive their records instead of deleting them; you may need their history later.
  • Be able to export. Whatever you use, make sure you can get your records out as a file (a CSV opens in any spreadsheet).
  • Limit who can see it. Attendance is student information. Share access with the staff who need it, not the whole parent group chat.

Paper, spreadsheet or app?

OptionWorks well whenWatch out for
Paper sheetYou're just starting, or need something by the door that never needs a charged battery.Looking up last month means flipping through binders. Sheets get lost or damp.
SpreadsheetOne person records everything and is comfortable with formulas.Two people editing at once, accidental deletions, and no record of who changed what.
Attendance appMore than one staff member takes attendance, or you want history and exports without extra work.Cost and setup. Many school systems are priced for much larger schools.

Plenty of small schools use paper at the door and an app as the record: the sheet is the morning tool, and someone enters it before the day ends. If you have a sheet photo, keep it with the day.
