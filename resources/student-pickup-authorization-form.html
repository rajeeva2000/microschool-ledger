Pickup safety guide

Student pickup authorization: a simple policy and a free printable form

By Microschool Ledger November 2026 5 min read

In a small school, staff usually know every parent by sight. That's exactly why a written pickup list matters: the day a grandparent, a neighbor or a new carpool driver shows up, nobody should have to guess. This guide covers what a pickup policy needs, how to handle the exceptions, and includes a free authorization form families can fill in at enrollment.

Why a written pickup list

  • Every staff member gives the same answer. A substitute or a volunteer at the door can check the list instead of making a judgment call.
  • Families decide, in advance and in writing. Nobody is put on the spot at the door.
  • It protects children in hard situations. When there's a custody arrangement or someone who shouldn't pick a child up, the school knows before it matters.

What your pickup policy should say

Keep it short enough that families actually read it. Most small-school policies cover:

  • Who may pick up: parents or guardians, plus the adults each family lists on their authorization form.
  • ID checks: staff ask for photo ID from anyone they don't recognize, every time, even if that person is on the list.
  • Changes in writing: families update their list in writing (email or a new form), not by telling a teacher in the hallway.
  • Same-day exceptions: how a parent tells you someone new is coming today, and how you confirm it (see below).
  • Court orders: if a court order limits who may pick up a child, the family gives the school a copy, and the school follows it.
  • Older students leaving on their own: whether it's allowed, at what age, and with whose written permission.
  • Late pickup: how long staff will wait, who they call, and any late-pickup charge.

Same-day changes, late pickups and emergencies

Someone new is picking up today

Ask families to tell you in writing before pickup, using a method you can check (an email or text from a number already on file). Then confirm the person's photo ID at the door. If you can't reach a parent to confirm, the child waits with staff.

Nobody has arrived

Call the parents first, then the adults on the authorization form, in order. Write down the time and who you reached.

Someone not on the list insists

Stay calm, keep the child with staff, and call the parent. A clear written policy makes this easier: you're following the school's rule, not refusing a person.

Recording who picked up

The authorization form says who may pick up. Your attendance record should say who did, at least for early pickups: the time and the adult's name.
