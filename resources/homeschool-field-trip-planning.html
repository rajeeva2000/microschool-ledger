Field trip guide

How to plan a homeschool co-op field trip without the group-text chaos

By Microschool Ledger November 2026 7 min read

Field trips are the part of co-op life families remember. They're also where a lot of admin time disappears: one announcement, forty replies in a group text, a sign-up sheet, a list of who still owes $12, and a headcount you're still guessing at the night before. This guide walks through a field trip from first idea to the bus ride home, with the decisions to make up front so the rest goes smoothly.

A field trip timeline

RSVP-by dateClose RSVPs. Confirm your final count with the venue and pay any deposit.
1 week outSend final details: meeting place and time, parking, lunch, what to wear, the weather plan.

Five decisions to make before you announce

Most field trip confusion comes from questions nobody answered in the first message. Settle these before you send anything:

  1. Who's invited? Every enrolled family, one class or age group, or a smaller set? Decide before you announce, not after.
  2. How many spots? If the venue has a cap, say so, and decide whether late RSVPs go on a waitlist.
  3. What does it cost, and per whom? Per student, per person, or a flat family price? Do younger siblings and adults pay? A clear rule up front saves a dozen individual conversations.
  4. How and when do families pay? Cash on the day, check, Venmo, or paid to the school ahead of time. Name a pay-by date.
  5. What's your cancellation policy? If the venue keeps your deposit, families should know whether a cancellation is refunded.
Tip

Put all five answers in the first announcement. Every one you leave out becomes a reply-all question.

Collecting RSVPs families actually answer

The goal is a clear yes or no from every invited family by the RSVP-by date, with the number of children and adults coming. A few things make that far more likely:

  • Ask one family at a time, not the whole group. Replies in a group thread get buried; a personal invite gets answered.
  • Make "no" easy. A "can't make it" answer is as useful as a yes. It tells you to stop reminding that family.
  • Ask which children are coming, not just "how many." It matters for name tags, age-based pricing and the headcount on the day.
  • Remind only the families who haven't answered. Reminding everyone trains families to ignore reminders.
  • Let families change their answer without messaging you. Plans change; you just want your list to stay current.
  • Keep one list. One place shows who's coming, who said no, who hasn't answered and who's on the waitlist.

Collecting money

Small trips usually aren't worth a payment system. What matters is knowing who has paid and how much is still owed, without keeping it in your head.

  • Show the cost with the RSVP, so a family's yes and what they owe arrive together.
  • Mark each payment when it comes in, with the amount. "The Riveras paid" is less useful than "the Riveras paid $24 for two children."
  • Watch for changes after payment. If a family adds a child after paying, they now owe more. Your list should show the difference.
  • Decide what happens to extra money (a family that overpaid, or a trip that cost less than planned) before it happens.

When field trip money becomes tuition money

If your school already bills families for tuition, field trip charges belong on the same family account so there's one balance, not two. That's what the full Microschool Ledger billing plan does, including online payment. For trips on their own, a simple paid/unpaid list is plenty.

Chaperones, siblings and headcounts

  • Count students and adults separately. Venues price them differently, and you need the ratio for supervision.
  • Set an adult limit per family if space is tight, and say so in the announcement.
  • Decide on siblings who aren't enrolled. Many co-ops welcome them, but they change the count and sometimes the cost.
  • Assign chaperones to small groups before the day, and give each chaperone their group's names.
  • Collect any forms ahead of time. If the venue or your school requires a permission slip or waiver, collect it with the RSVP rather than at the bus.

Day-before checklist

  • Final attendee list: families, students, adults, and anyone on the waitlist
  • Who has paid and who still owes
  • Venue confirmation, with your contact person's name and phone number
  • Meeting place, arrival time and parking details sent to families
  • Weather or cancellation plan sent to families
  • Chaperone groups assigned
  • Allergy and medical notes for the students attending
  • Emergency contacts for every student attending
  • Permission slips or waivers collected
  • A headcount method for the day: name list, wristbands or group leaders
